How the Texas foreclosure process works

Updated 2026-11-09 · Educational guide, not legal advice

Most Texas mortgages are foreclosed without a lawsuit, using the power of sale in the deed of trust. For a residence homestead the lender must first give at least 20 days to cure the default; then notice of sale must be posted and filed at least 21 days before the auction, which is held on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

Why most Texas foreclosures skip the courthouse lawsuit

Texas home loans are usually secured by a deed of trust that gives a trustee the power to sell the property if the borrower defaults. Property Code Section 51.002 sets the rules for that sale. Because no lawsuit is needed, the process can move from default to auction in a matter of weeks once the notices are sent.

Step 1: Notice of default and chance to cure (homesteads)

If the property is the debtor's residence homestead, the lender must send written notice by certified mail that the debtor is in default and give at least 20 days to cure before the notice of sale can be given.

Step 2: Notice of sale, at least 21 days ahead

At least 21 days before the sale, notice must be:

  • posted at the courthouse door of each county where the property is located,
  • filed in the office of the county clerk of each of those counties, and
  • sent to the debtor.

The statute also provides for posting on the county's website in counties that maintain one for this purpose.

Step 3: The first-Tuesday auction

The sale happens on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. (the first Wednesday if the Tuesday is January 1 or July 4). It takes place at the county courthouse in the county where the land sits, or in an alternative area the commissioners court has designated. The notice states the county and the earliest time the sale will begin.

How buying at a Texas foreclosure auction works

  • Bring certified funds. Trustees typically require payment at or soon after the sale. Ask the trustee named in the notice for the terms.
  • You are buying what the lender's lien covered. A foreclosure of a junior lien does not wipe out a senior mortgage. Pull title, or pay a title company for a report, before the sale.
  • Property is usually sold as is. You generally cannot inspect inside, and the home may be occupied.
  • Many sales are cancelled or postponed. Borrowers reinstate, sell, or file bankruptcy. Confirm with the trustee the morning of the sale.

Frequently asked questions

Is Texas a judicial or nonjudicial foreclosure state?

Most Texas home loans are secured by a deed of trust with a power of sale, so foreclosure is usually nonjudicial: a trustee or substitute trustee sells the property at auction under Property Code Section 51.002 without a court case.

When are Texas foreclosure auctions held?

On the first Tuesday of each month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., or the first Wednesday if the first Tuesday is January 1 or July 4, at the county courthouse or another area the commissioners court designates.

How much notice is required before a Texas foreclosure sale?

At least 21 days. The notice is posted at the courthouse door, filed with the county clerk, and the debtor is also sent notice. For a residence homestead, the debtor must first receive a written notice of default by certified mail giving at least 20 days to cure.

Where do I find upcoming foreclosure notices?

Notices of sale are filed with the county clerk, and many county clerks post them online. The county pages on this site link to each county's official website.
