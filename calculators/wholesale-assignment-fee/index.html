Wholesale assignment fee calculator

A wholesale assignment fee is the difference between what an end buyer will pay for your contract and your contract price with the seller. If the buyer's maximum is ARV x 70% - repairs, the most you can charge is that amount minus your contract price.

How is a wholesale assignment fee calculated?

  1. End buyer's maximum price = ARV x the buyer's rule percentage - repairs
  2. Maximum assignment fee = end buyer's maximum price - your contract price
  3. Net fee = maximum assignment fee - your costs

The buyer's percentage is usually the same 70% used in the 70% rule, but buyers who plan to keep the house as a rental may pay more or less.

Worked example

With the example values: ARV $250,000, repairs $40,000, buyer percentage 70%, and a contract price of $125,000.

  • End buyer's maximum: $135,000
  • Maximum assignment fee: $135,000 - $125,000 = $10,000
  • After $2,000 of your own costs: $8,000

The fee is only as good as the ARV and repair estimates. Buyers will check both, so price them honestly or the assignment will not close.

Know the rules before you market a contract

This calculator is for the math only. Before you advertise or assign a contract, check the current requirements with the Texas Real Estate Commission or the Oklahoma Real Estate Commission.

Frequently asked questions

Is wholesaling legal in Texas and Oklahoma?

This calculator covers the math, not the law. Rules on marketing and assigning a contract for property you do not own are set by each state, so read the current guidance from the Texas Real Estate Commission or the Oklahoma Real Estate Commission and talk to a real estate attorney before you market a contract.

Why subtract my costs?

Marketing to find the seller, earnest money you could lose, and title or attorney fees all come out of the fee. The net figure is what you actually keep.

What if the fee comes out negative?

Then your contract price is above what a cash buyer would pay at that rule percentage, and the deal will be hard to assign. Either renegotiate the price or find a buyer with a different strategy, such as a landlord.
