How does the 70% rule work?

Maximum offer = ARV x 70% - estimated repairs. ARV is what the house should sell for once fixed, based on recent sales of similar updated homes nearby. Repairs are your full rehab estimate.

The rule is fast because it needs only two numbers. That speed is its value when you are screening many leads; its weakness is that it hides the costs it is supposed to cover.

Worked example

With the example values, ARV is {{rule70.in.arv|money}} and repairs are {{rule70.in.repairs|money}}:

  • {{rule70.in.arv|money}} x {{rule70.in.pct|num}}% = {{rule70.arv_pct|money}}
  • {{rule70.arv_pct|money}} - {{rule70.in.repairs|money}} = {{rule70.max_offer|money}} maximum offer

To check whether that offer really leaves a profit, put {{rule70.max_offer|money}} into the fix and flip calculator with your actual loan, holding and selling costs.

How wholesalers use the rule

Wholesalers usually work backward from the 70% number, because it is roughly what a cash buyer will pay. The difference between that number and the wholesaler's contract price is the room for an assignment fee. The wholesale fee calculator does that math.
