Who can sell an Oklahoma property after the owner dies?

Real estate that was in the deceased owner's name alone generally needs a court process before it can be sold with insurable title. In Oklahoma, probate cases are filed in the district court of the county where the person lived. Either the court-appointed personal representative sells the property with the court's authority, or the court's final order distributes it to the heirs, who then sell as owners.

Summary administration for smaller estates

Title 58, Section 245 allows a petition for summary administration when the estate's value is $200,000 or less and other conditions in the statute are met. Summary administration is designed to finish faster than a regular probate, which matters to a buyer waiting on a closing.

The small estate affidavit

Section 393 lets a successor collect a decedent's property by affidavit, starting 10 or more days after death, when the decedent's Oklahoma property (less liens and encumbrances) is worth $50,000 or less. The section speaks of paying or delivering property to the successor and specifically addresses severed mineral interests. Do not assume it can transfer title to a house or land; ask a title company or attorney before relying on it.

Practical checklist for buyers

  • Find the probate case in the district court records and read the latest orders.
  • Confirm who has authority to sign, and whether the court must approve the sale.
  • Open title with a title company early and ask what it needs to insure.
  • Check the county treasurer for delinquent taxes; after three years of non-payment the land can go to the June resale (see the Oklahoma tax sale guide).
  • Budget for deferred maintenance with the fix and flip calculator.

This is general information about Oklahoma law, not legal advice. Questions about a specific estate belong with an Oklahoma probate attorney.
