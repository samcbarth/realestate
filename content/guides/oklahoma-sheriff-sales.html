Step by step: from judgment to sheriff's deed

  1. Judgment. The district court enters a foreclosure judgment and orders the property sold (see the Oklahoma foreclosure guide).
  2. Appraisal. Unless the mortgage waived appraisement, the sheriff has the property appraised by three disinterested people, according to OKLaw.org's summary of the process.
  3. Notice. Notice of the sale is published. The sale cannot take place less than 30 days after the first publication (Title 12, Section 764).
  4. Auction. The sheriff sells to the highest bidder. If the property was appraised, the bid must be at least two-thirds of the appraised value.
  5. Confirmation. Notice of the confirmation hearing goes to the owners, and the court decides whether to confirm the sale. Only then does the buyer receive the sheriff's deed.

Appraisal waived? Expect a six-month wait

Some mortgages include the words "and waive the appraisement". Then the property is sold without an appraisal, which removes the two-thirds minimum, but Title 12, Section 760 bars the order of sale until six months after the judgment.

How to find Oklahoma sheriff sales

Each county sheriff publishes its own list and bidding rules. Some post them online, such as the Payne County Sheriff's Office and Lincoln County Sheriff. For other counties, start from our county pages, which link the official county websites. Cases also appear in the district court records before the sale is set.

Pre-bid checklist

  • Get the appraised value from the notice; your bid must be at least two-thirds of it.
  • Read the court file for other parties and liens.
  • Learn the sheriff's deposit and payment terms.
  • Remember the sale still needs court confirmation; plan your financing timeline around it.
  • Set a ceiling with the 70% rule calculator before the auction.
