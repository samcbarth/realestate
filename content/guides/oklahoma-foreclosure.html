Two paths to an Oklahoma foreclosure sale

1. Judicial foreclosure (the usual path)

The lender files suit in district court. Under Title 12, Section 686, the court enters judgment for the amount due and orders the property sold, with the proceeds applied to the debt. The sale is then carried out by the county sheriff. See the Oklahoma sheriff sale guide for how the auction itself works.

Two timing rules matter to buyers watching a case:

  • Notice of the sale is published, and the sale cannot be held less than 30 days after the first publication (Title 12, Section 764).
  • If the mortgage waived appraisement, no order of sale can issue for six months after the judgment (Title 12, Section 760). In exchange, the property is sold without the appraisal minimum.

2. Power of sale (nonjudicial)

The Oklahoma Power of Sale Mortgage Foreclosure Act (Title 46, Sections 40-49) allows a sale without a lawsuit when the mortgage grants a power of sale. Key steps:

  • Notice of intent to foreclose. Sent by certified mail; the mortgagor has 35 days from the date it is sent to cure the default (Section 44).
  • Notice of sale. Served on the mortgagor and others with recorded interests, and published, before the sale date (Section 45).
  • Homestead election. If the property is the mortgagor's homestead, the mortgagor can send notice by certified mail at least 10 days before the sale electing judicial foreclosure and record it with the county clerk. The lender must then go to court (Section 43). A homestead owner can also elect against a deficiency judgment the same way.
  • Redemption until the sale is complete. The mortgagor or another party may redeem by paying the amount owed, including expenses, up to the completion of the sale (Section 43).

What this means for investors

  • Because most cases are judicial, upcoming foreclosures appear in the district court's case records long before the sale. That gives you time to research the property.
  • After a sheriff's sale, the court holds a confirmation hearing. The sale is not final until it is confirmed.
  • Compare with Texas, where most sales happen within weeks of notice: see the Texas foreclosure guide or the side-by-side comparison.
