How Oklahoma's tax sale system works

Oklahoma does not auction land the first year taxes go unpaid. Under Title 68, Section 3105, real property whose taxes have been unpaid for three years or more, counted from when they first became due and payable, is sold at the tax resale described in Section 3125.

When is the resale held?

Section 3125 sets the resale on the second Monday of June each year in each county. Counties that use an online auction start it on a date and time the county treasurer sets during normal business hours within the full week of the second Monday of June, and it runs until the sale concludes.

Who runs it and where to find the list

The county treasurer runs the resale. Treasurers publish the list of properties and the bidding rules ahead of the sale. Start with the treasurer's office for the county you are interested in; our county pages link to each county's official website and treasurer where available. Many Oklahoma counties also post tax roll searches through their treasurer.

What changed from the old certificate sale

If you read older investor material, you will see an October tax certificate sale. The current statute text ties the sale of delinquent land to the June resale, so older guides describing certificate purchases do not describe today's process. Verify the current year's procedure with the treasurer before you plan around it.

Before you bid at an Oklahoma resale

  • Get the treasurer's published terms: registration, deposit, payment deadline, and what kind of deed the county issues.
  • Check the county records for liens and for any notice issues that could affect the deed.
  • Ask a title company or real estate attorney whether a resale deed will be insurable and what steps, if any, are needed afterward.
  • Visit the property; resale land is often vacant lots or homes needing work.
  • Estimate next year's taxes with the property tax calculator.

Oklahoma amended parts of its delinquent tax statutes recently. Read the current text of Title 68, Article 31, or ask the county treasurer, before relying on any detail not listed here.
